QUESTION 1 — Supply Chain Attacks
Category: TPCRM fundamentals

What is a supply chain attack?

A) An attack that targets an organization's physical logistics and shipping operations
B) An attack in which cyber criminals infiltrate a target through an outside partner or provider that has access to the target's systems, network, or data
C) An attack that exclusively targets manufacturing control systems
D) A denial-of-service attack against a company's suppliers

Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Supply chain attacks succeed by compromising an outside partner or provider that has trusted access to the target's systems, network, or data — turning that trusted connection into the attack path.

QUESTION 2 — SolarWinds
Category: TPCRM fundamentals (real-world example)

In the SolarWinds attack, approximately how many organizations and customers were affected after attackers injected malware into the company's software?

A) Around 25 organizations and 1,800 customers
B) Around 250 organizations and 18,000 customers
C) Around 2,500 organizations and 180,000 customers
D) Around 100 organizations and 5,000 customers

Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Over 250 organizations and more than 18,000 customers were affected after attackers injected malware into the SolarWinds "Orion" software — the textbook example of how one successful supply chain compromise cascades into a successful attack against many.

QUESTION 3 — TPCRM Definition
Category: TPCRM fundamentals

Third-Party Cyber Risk Management (TPCRM) is best described as:

A) A set of contracts that transfers all cyber risk to vendors
B) A one-time vendor audit conducted before signing a contract
C) An organized approach to analyzing, controlling, monitoring, and mitigating cyber risks associated with third-party vendors, suppliers, and service providers
D) A certification that vendors must obtain before working with an organization

Correct Answer: C
Explanation: TPCRM is an organized, ongoing approach covering analysis, control, monitoring, and mitigation of cyber risks tied to third-party vendors, suppliers, and service providers. It is not a one-time activity and not something that can be contractually transferred away.

================================================================

QUESTION 4 — Inherent vs Residual Risk
Category: Risk concepts

A vendor has been assessed and several security controls are now in place. The risks that STILL remain after all those controls have been accounted for are referred to as:

A) Inherent risk
B) Residual risk
C) Accepted risk
D) Transferred risk

Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Residual risk is what remains after security controls have been applied. Inherent risk, by contrast, is the risk present just by dealing with the third party — before any controls are considered.

QUESTION 5 — 6 Steps of TPCRM
Category: TPCRM process

You are setting up a TPCRM program. Which of the following represents the correct early-stage ordering?

A) Assign ratings → Identify vendors → Evaluate risk → Send questionnaires
B) Identify vendors → Evaluate risk (classify by inherent risk) → Send questionnaires to determine residual risk → Assign ratings
C) Send questionnaires → Identify vendors → Assign ratings → Evaluate risk
D) Continuous monitoring → Identify vendors → Assign ratings → Send questionnaires

Correct Answer: B
Explanation: The 6 steps begin with vendor identification, then risk evaluation based on inherent risk, then questionnaires to determine residual risk, followed by assigning ratings, addressing risks by priority, and finally continuous monitoring.

QUESTION 6 — Risk Ratings and Mitigation Timelines
Category: TPCRM process

A vendor risk is classified as HIGH. According to the TPCRM scorecard approach, how should it be handled?

A) Accept the risk or deploy mitigation steps over an extended period of time
B) Deploy mitigation steps within a stipulated time frame
C) Deploy mitigation steps immediately
D) Wait until the next annual reassessment to decide

Correct Answer: C
Explanation: High risks require immediate mitigation. Medium risks are mitigated within a stipulated time frame, and low risks may be accepted or mitigated over an extended period of time.

QUESTION 7 — Point-in-Time Assessments
Category: Continuous monitoring

Why is relying solely on annual questionnaires to manage third-party cyber risk considered insufficient?

A) Questionnaires are too expensive to send out annually
B) Vendors are not required to respond to questionnaires
C) A questionnaire freezes a moment in time while vendor posture changes daily, meaning a snapshot from months ago will not reflect today's zero-days or new exposures
D) Questionnaires are not aligned with any regulatory frameworks

Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Point-in-time assessments produce a snapshot, not reality. Vendor posture changes daily as new CVEs, misconfigurations, and breaches emerge — which is why continuous monitoring must augment (not replace) periodic assessments.

QUESTION 8 — Continuous Monitoring Signals
Category: Continuous monitoring

Which of the following is NOT typically a signal used in continuous third-party monitoring?

A) External attack surface data (exposed services, open ports, cloud misconfigurations, SSL/TLS issues)
B) Threat intelligence feeds (active campaign mentions, breach database hits, dark web credential leaks)
C) Security ratings platforms (BitSight, SecurityScorecard, UpGuard, Panorays)
D) The vendor's annual marketing brochure

Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Modern continuous monitoring draws on external attack surface data, threat intelligence feeds, and security ratings platforms. Marketing materials are not a security signal.

QUESTION 9 — SBOM Definition
Category: SBOMs

What is a Software Bill of Materials (SBOM)?

A) A financial invoice sent from a software vendor to a customer
B) A machine-readable inventory of every component, library, and dependency inside a piece of software
C) A user manual describing the features of a software product
D) A contract defining licensing obligations between software providers

Correct Answer: B
Explanation: An SBOM is a machine-readable inventory (typically JSON or XML) of components, versions, licenses, and dependencies inside a piece of software. It works like a food ingredients label — it tells you exactly what is inside.

QUESTION 10 — SBOM Formats
Category: SBOMs

Which two SBOM formats are the main standards in use today?

A) PDF and DOCX
B) SPDX (Linux Foundation, ISO/IEC 5962:2021) and CycloneDX (OWASP)
C) YAML and TOML
D) PNG and SVG

Correct Answer: B
Explanation: SPDX, backed by the Linux Foundation and standardized as ISO/IEC 5962:2021, and CycloneDX, an OWASP standard, are the two dominant SBOM formats. Both are machine-readable and integrate with CI/CD pipelines; the choice depends on ecosystem and tooling preference.
