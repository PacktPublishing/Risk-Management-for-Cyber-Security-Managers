QUESTION 1 — What is Risk (Cybersecurity Definition)
Category: Risk definition

In cybersecurity, risk is most accurately defined as:

A) The total number of cyberattacks an organization faces in a year
B) The likelihood that a vulnerability will be exploited by a threat
C) The financial cost of implementing security controls
D) The probability that an employee will make a mistake

Correct Answer: B
Explanation: In cybersecurity, risk is the likelihood that a vulnerability (a weakness in the security system or setup) will be exploited by a threat. Reducing either threats or vulnerabilities lowers risk.

QUESTION 2 — Risk Definitions Compared (Scenario)
Category: Risk definition

SCENARIO: A CISO tells the board: "Our risk is that an unpatched server will be exploited by ransomware operators." A board member asks how this definition compares to the ISO definition of risk. What's the best answer?

A) They mean the same thing
B) The CISO's definition is the cybersecurity-specific framing (threat + vulnerability), while ISO defines risk more broadly as the effect of uncertainty on objectives
C) The ISO definition only applies to financial risk
D) The CISO's definition is wrong — risk is always defined as financial loss

Correct Answer: B
Explanation: The cybersecurity definition frames risk as the likelihood a vulnerability will be exploited by a threat. The ISO definition is broader — "effect of uncertainty on objectives" — focusing on how incomplete knowledge affects organizational decision-making.

QUESTION 3 — Types of Risk: Operational
Category: Types of risk

Which of the following is an example of Operational Risk?

A) A new law requires companies to disclose breaches within 72 hours
B) A power outage takes down the data center, and employees fail to follow the backup procedure
C) A competitor releases a new product that takes market share
D) A customer publicly complains about a product defect on social media

Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Operational risk refers to risks from internal failures in people or systems — employees failing to follow security policies and power outages are textbook examples. The other options describe compliance, strategic, and reputational risks respectively.

================================================================

QUESTION 4 — Types of Risk: Compliance (Scenario)
Category: Types of risk

SCENARIO: A publicly traded company fails to maintain accurate financial records as required by Sarbanes-Oxley. Which type of risk is the company primarily exposed to?

A) Operational Risk
B) Strategic Risk
C) Compliance Risk
D) Reputational Risk

Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Compliance risk refers to exposure to legal penalties and material loss from failing to comply with industry laws and regulations. Sarbanes-Oxley applies to companies with publicly traded stock, and violations fall squarely under compliance risk.

QUESTION 5 — Types of Risk: Reputational
Category: Types of risk

Which scenario best illustrates Reputational Risk?

A) A credit card processor experiences a temporary outage during a holiday sale
B) A car manufacturer faces a major recall due to product defects, and media coverage causes customers to switch to competitors
C) A company's server room loses power due to a storm
D) An employee forgets to apply a critical security patch

Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Reputational risk is the loss of a company's reputation from events like data breaches, product failures, lawsuits, or negative publicity. A recall that drives customers to competitors is a classic reputational risk.

QUESTION 6 — Types of Risk: Strategic vs. Financial
Category: Types of risk

An investment firm allows clients to invest in cryptocurrency but may change this offering if a new form of digital money emerges. This is an example of:

A) Financial Risk
B) Strategic Risk
C) Operational Risk
D) Compliance Risk

Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Strategic risk refers to risks associated with operations in a specific industry within a specific period of time. The firm's decision to adjust its offering in response to changes in the industry is a strategic risk decision, not financial (which deals with how payments are handled).

QUESTION 7 — Threats
Category: Threat actors

Which of the following is NOT one of the threat actor categories covered in this section?

A) Black Hats
B) Hacktivists
C) Script Kiddies
D) Nation States

Correct Answer: C
Explanation: The threat actors covered in this section are Black Hats, Hacktivists, Nation States, Employees, Disgruntled Ex-Employees, and Insiders/Saboteurs. Script kiddies — though real-world threat actors — are not covered in this lesson.

QUESTION 8 — Threats (Scenario)
Category: Threat actors

SCENARIO: A terminated employee still has active VPN credentials and uses them from home to delete critical files from the company's shared drive. Which threat actor category best describes this?

A) Black Hat
B) Nation State
C) Disgruntled Ex-Employee
D) Hacktivist

Correct Answer: C
Explanation: A disgruntled ex-employee uses leftover access (often due to poor offboarding) to cause harm. This is distinct from an insider (still employed), a black hat (external hacker for profit), or a hacktivist (ideologically motivated).

QUESTION 9 — Risk Management Roles
Category: Risk management roles

Which role is primarily responsible for making the final decisions regarding risk management?

A) Risk Manager
B) Risk Management Specialists
C) Subject Matter Experts
D) Risk Owner

Correct Answer: D
Explanation: The Risk Owner — which can be an individual, a group of people, or even another company — is primarily responsible for making the final decisions about how a risk is managed. The Risk Manager establishes the framework and leads; specialists execute; SMEs provide identification recommendations.

QUESTION 10 — Risk Management Types (Scenario)
Category: Risk management types

SCENARIO: Your security team holds a workshop where they ask "What could go wrong with our customer database?" and capture every possible failure scenario on a whiteboard. Which type of risk management activity is this?

A) Compliance Risk Management
B) Risk Management Sessions
C) Technical / Vulnerability Risk Management
D) Risk Management by Threat Monitoring

Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Risk Management Sessions are brainstorming sessions that ask what could possibly go wrong with assets. Compliance risk management uses pre-established criteria (checklists); technical/vulnerability focuses on OS flaws; threat monitoring watches the environment for incoming threats.
