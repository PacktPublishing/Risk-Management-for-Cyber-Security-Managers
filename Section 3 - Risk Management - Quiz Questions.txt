QUESTION 1 — Risk Analysis Steps
Category: Risk analysis process

Which of the following is NOT one of the risk analysis steps covered in this section?

A) Identify assets
B) Identify vulnerabilities
C) Publish a press release
D) Determine safeguards and countermeasures

Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Risk analysis steps are: identify assets (scoping), identify vulnerabilities, identify exploits & threats, determine safeguards/countermeasures, and decide which risks are acceptable. Press releases are PR, not risk analysis.

QUESTION 2 — CIA Triad
Category: Risk identification

Which element of the CIA Triad is compromised when data has been altered without authorization?

A) Confidentiality
B) Integrity
C) Availability
D) Accessibility

Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Integrity means data has not been altered. Confidentiality concerns whether only authorized users access the data; Availability concerns whether data is accessible when needed.

QUESTION 3 — Risk Register (Scenario)
Category: Risk identification

SCENARIO: A risk manager is building a risk register entry for the threat of phishing attacks compromising user passwords. Which of the following is NOT a standard risk register feature covered in this section?

A) Risk Statement and Risk ID
B) Asset and Risk Rating
C) Mitigation Plan and Cost of Mitigation
D) Attacker's real name and IP address

Correct Answer: D
Explanation: A risk register documents Risk Statement, Risk ID, Asset, Risk Rating, Risk Treatment, Mitigation/Transfer Plan, Acceptance/Approval, Risk Impact, Risk Cause, and Cost of Mitigation. Attacker identity isn't a risk register field.

QUESTION 4 — Likelihood Rating
Category: Risk assessment

Using the likelihood rating scale in this section, an event expected to occur every month would be rated as:

A) Probably (3)
B) Likely (4)
C) Very Likely (5)
D) Very Unlikely (1)

Correct Answer: C
Explanation: The scale: Very Unlikely (once in 5 years), Unlikely (once in 3 years), Probably (once a year), Likely (a few times a year), Very Likely (every month).

QUESTION 5 — Risk Rating Matrix (Scenario)
Category: Risk assessment

SCENARIO: Your organization assesses a risk with a Likelihood of 5 (Very Likely) and an Impact of 4 (High). Using the risk rating matrix, how is this risk classified?

A) Low Risk
B) Medium Risk
C) High Risk
D) Unrated

Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Likelihood 5 × Impact 4 falls in the High Risk zone on the matrix. The scoring bands are: 1–7 Low, 7–17 Medium, 17–25 High.

QUESTION 6 — Mitigation vs. Avoidance (Scenario)
Category: Risk response

SCENARIO: To address the risk of employees using personal devices for work, a company implements a strict "No BYOD" policy banning all personal devices from the corporate network. This is an example of:

A) Risk Mitigation
B) Risk Avoidance
C) Risk Transference
D) Risk Acceptance

Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Banning BYOD eliminates the risk entirely rather than reducing its likelihood — that's avoidance. Mitigation would be allowing BYOD but with MDM, encryption, etc. The key distinction: mitigation reduces risk to a minimum; avoidance eliminates it completely.

QUESTION 7 — Risk Transference (Scenario)
Category: Risk response

SCENARIO: An organization can't afford to build the flood-proofing infrastructure needed to protect its data center, so it purchases a cyber insurance policy that covers flood-related data loss. This is an example of:

A) Risk Mitigation
B) Risk Avoidance
C) Risk Transference
D) Risk Acceptance

Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Risk transference assigns the risk (or its financial consequences) to another entity — commonly through insurance. It's typically used when the organization cannot mitigate the risk on its own due to cost.

QUESTION 8 — Risk Acceptance vs. Risk Rejection
Category: Risk response

What distinguishes Risk Rejection from Risk Acceptance?

A) Risk rejection means the risk is transferred; acceptance means it's mitigated
B) Risk acceptance acknowledges a valid risk whose mitigation cost outweighs the loss; risk rejection means management believes the risk is invalid
C) They mean exactly the same thing
D) Risk rejection is used for compliance risks; acceptance is for operational risks

Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Risk acceptance is a deliberate decision to bear a valid risk because mitigation costs more than the potential loss. Risk rejection is different — it means management has judged the identified risk to be invalid or not a real risk worth responding to.

QUESTION 9 — Risk Appetite
Category: Risk management concepts

Risk Appetite refers to:

A) The specific dollar amount a company sets aside for security incidents
B) A statement describing how much risk an organization is willing to accept
C) The technical capacity of an organization's security tools
D) The number of risks listed in the risk register

Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Risk appetite is an organization's statement of how much risk it is willing to accept. It varies between organizations based on budget, values, and the type of data they handle.

QUESTION 10 — Risk Monitoring (Scenario)
Category: Risk monitoring

SCENARIO: Five years ago, phishing emails from your vendors were rare and easy to spot. Today, your team is seeing AI-generated voice phishing (vishing) and targeted sextortion attempts. Why does this illustrate the importance of Risk Monitoring?

A) Because threats are static and predictable once identified
B) Because risks identified once should be removed from the register after a year
C) Because threats constantly evolve, requiring periodic re-evaluation and re-identification
D) Because monitoring replaces the need for mitigation

Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Risk monitoring exists because threats are constantly evolving — phishing has progressed from simple emails to vishing and sextortion. This makes periodic reviews, re-evaluation, and re-identification of risks essential.
